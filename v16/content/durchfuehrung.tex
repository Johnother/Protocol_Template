\section{Experimental setup and procedure}
\label{sec:Durchführung}
The experimental setup and the experimental procedure are discussed in this section. 
\subsection{Experimental setup}
The experimental setup is placed inside a vacuum chamber. %, so that the alpha particles can reach the foil and the detector, when the chamber is evacuated. 
A rotary vane pump is used for this purpose. 
Inside the chamber is a mount for the radioactive source and for the different foils. 
Between those two is a collimator, so that the beam of alpha particles is not divergent.
When the foil is attached to the mount, its height can be adjusted via an electric motor, so that the measurements can be taken with and without the foil. 
The surface barrier detector is mounted so that it can be adjusted to different scattering angles via an electric motor. 
This internal setup is shown in \autoref{fig:setup}.
The controls for both electric motors are outside the vacuum chamber. 
The set angle is displayed on the control panel for the motors. 
The zero point can be changed manually.
The signal output of the detector goes through a pre-amplifier and after that another amplifier bevor it goes in the counter.
The signal output of the detector is also connected to an oscilloscope. 
The counter is connected to a timer, where the time for the measurement can be set. 
The detector is powered by an external power supply outside of the vacuum chamber. 
The vacuum chamber can be darkened with a cover, which is necessary because the detector is very sensitive to ambient light, as explained in \autoref{subsec:detector}.

\subsection{Experimental Procedure}
First of all the dector pulse signal is oberserved with the oscilloscope both before and after the amplifier. 
\newline
After that the threshold for the counter has to be set. 
Therefore the radioactive source has to be inserted in the experimental setup.
Then a measurement is taken when the vacuum chamber is not evacuated but darkened, so the alpha particles cannot reach the detector but the background from gamma and beta radiation can. 
This is because of the low range of alpha particles in air, as mentioned in \autoref{subsec:alpha_decay}.
Then the threshold is set so that there are almost no events registered with this setup.
In doing so, the registered background events are minimized,
because the resulting current in the detector is proportional to the energy of the incoming particle, as explained in \autoref{subsec:detector}.
The background mainly consists of the gamma radiation, which was mentioned in \autoref{subsec:alpha_decay}.
\newline
Next the gold ($Z=79$) foil is mounted on its mount and the vacuum chamber is evacuated. 
Now the alpha particles can reach the detector. 
Then the zero point for the detector's angle has to be callibrated. 
Therefore the height of the foil is set, so that it is not in the way of the alpha particle beam. 
Measurements are taken for the angles $-5 \textdegree$ to $+5 \textdegree$ around the preset zero point in steps of $1 \textdegree$.
If necessary the zero point is adjusted to the angle which had the highest count rate.
\newline
After that the measurements for the gold foil can be taken. 
In order to do so, the gold foil is lowered, so that the beam intersects the foil. 
Then the count rate is measured for different scattering angles starting at $0\textdegree$.
For that the timer is set to a sufficently large time, so that the error is minimized. 
The goal is to reach at least $1112$ counts, to have an error smaller than $0.03$ according to \autoref{eqn:relative_poisson}.
In the range from $3\textdegree$ up to $8\textdegree$ the measurements are taken in steps of $1 \textdegree$. 
For larger angles the length of the steps is adjusted, as the count rate decreases. 
When the count rate is too low to minimize the error, there are no more measurements taken for higher scattering angles. 
%For each measurement the timer for the counter is set to a sufficiently long time, so that the count rate is high enough to minimize the statistical error. 
\newline
The same procedure is repeated with the bismuth ($Z=83$) foil and the aluminum ($Z=13$) foil.
