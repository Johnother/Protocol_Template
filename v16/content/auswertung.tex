\section{Results and Evaluation}
\label{sec:Auswertung}
Calculations with the resulting data are done digitally with the programming language \enquote{Python}.
Multiple packages are used, such as \enquote{Matplotlib}~\cite{matplotlib} for creating graphics, \enquote{SciPy}~\cite{scipy} for physics constants and curve-fitting and \enquote{Numpy}~\cite{numpy} for data manamgement and math support.
The uncertainty management as stated in \autoref{sec:fehlerrechnung} is done with the \enquote{Uncertainties}-package~\cite{uncertainties}.
\newline\noindent
The error for the scattering angle is determined by the resolution of the display of the rotor, which is accurate to $\increment\theta = \qty{0.1}{\degree}$.
As it is unknown how the rotor controller rounds the values inbetween, $\increment\theta = \pm\,\qty{0.1}{\degree}$ is taken as the absolute error for every scattering angle value.

\subsection{Characteristics of the detector signal pulses}
Before the detector pulses are counted by the digital counter, the signal is amplified.
Why this is necessary can be seen when looking at the not amplified and the amplified signal pulse.
Both are shown in \autoref{fig:pulses}.
\begin{figure}
  \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[height=5cm]{content/images/pre-amplified.jpg}
    \caption{Not amplified signal.}
    \label{subfig:pre}
  \end{subfigure}
  \hfill
  \begin{subfigure}{0.48\textwidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[height=5cm]{content/images/amplified.jpg}
    \caption{Amplified signal.}
    \label{subfig:after}
  \end{subfigure}
  \caption{Detector pulses before and after amplification, shown on a digital oscilloscope.}
  \label{fig:pulses}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier
\noindent
Before amplification the signal pulse is distorted by irregular signal noise, as \autoref{subfig:pre} shows.
Also, the pulse height is in the same range as the high-frequency noise, reaching $U_{\mathrm{pre}} \approx \qty{300}{\milli\volt}$. 
In the amplified signal the detector pulse is much more distinct, with a pulse height of $U_{\mathrm{post}} \approx \qty{11}{\volt}$, as shown in \autoref{subfig:after}.
The pulse is amplified by a preset factor of $V_{\mathrm{Amp}} = \qty{16.6}{}$.
To improve the visibility of the whole pulse, the timesteps on the oscilloscope are larger for the amplified pulse than for the unamplified signal.
The noise is still visible for the amplified signal, as the baseline before and after the pulse is distorted irregularly.

\subsection{Beam profile measurement for calibration}
The Rutherford-experiment depends on the scattering angle, with respect to the center of the incoming beam of $\alpha$-particles.
To ensure that the beam center is aligned correctly with the surface barrier detector, the undisturbed beam profile is measured.
\autoref{fig:calibration} shows the intensity of the $\alpha$-particles depending on the scattering angle without the foil in the way and under vacuum.
The data is taken from \autoref{tab:calibration} in the appendix.
\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{content/images/calibration.pdf}
  \caption{Angular dependence of the undisturbed beam of $\alpha$-particles.}
  \label{fig:calibration}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier
\noindent
The beam profile shows a gaussian-like shape as expected for an undisturbed beam of particles.
Although, the center of the beam is shifted from the center defined by the angle of the rotor by $\increment\theta = \qty{1}{\degree}$.
To calibrate the rotor, the new $\qty{0}{\degree}$-point is corrected by the observed shift.

\subsection{Alpha particle scattering on gold foil}
\label{subsec:Au2}
For the first scattering experiment a gold foil with a thickness of $d_{\mathrm{Au}} = \qty{2}{\micro\meter}$ is used.
Multiple scattering angles are measured, up to $\theta = \qty{15(0.1)}{\degree}$.
For every scattering angle the measurement time $\increment t$ as well as the vacuum chamber pressure $p$ after the measurement are documented.
The data is listed in \autoref{tab:Au2} in the appendix.
\newline\noindent
In \autoref{fig:Au2} the count rate is plotted against the scattering angle.
To check for the dependence given by \autoref{eqn:rutherford}, the data is fitted with a fit-function
\begin{equation}
  R\left(\theta\right) = \frac{A}{\sin{\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}^b}\,.
  \label{eqn:fit}
\end{equation}
The parameter $A$ accounts for the proportionality given in \autoref{eqn:RpropWQ}.
Its value is proportional to the scattering material's properties and therefore not relevant for the angular dependence.
The fit is realized by the function \textit{curve\_fit} from the \enquote{SciPy}-library, with default initial parameters $A=b=1$.
\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{content/images/Au2.pdf}
  \caption{Intensity of the $\alpha$-particles with respect to the scattering angle, scattered on $d_{\mathrm{Au}} = \qty{2}{\micro\meter}$ gold foil.}
  \label{fig:Au2}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier
\noindent
The data points for small scattering angles up to $\theta = \qty{5(0.1)}{\degree}$ are not fitted through because they lie inside the measured initial particle beam.
The $\theta=\qty{6(0.1)}{\degree}$-value is disregarded because of the large deviation from the expected curve.
Further discussion about the choice of data points for the fit functions in this and later measurements is done in \autoref{sec:Diskussion}.
\newline\noindent
The fit parameters returned by the fit-function are
\begin{align}
  A_{\mathrm{Au}} &= \qty{4.64(1.11)e-5}{\per\second}\,, & b_{\mathrm{Au}} &= \qty{4.05(0.09)}\,.
\end{align}
The uncertainties of the parameteres are calculated as the square root of the diagonal elements of the parameter's covariance matrix.

\subsection{Z-dependence of scattering intensity}
The Rutherford scattering formula takes into account the atomic number of the scattering material.
In this section the goal is to show that the count rate is proportional to the atomic number. 
\autoref{eqn:rutherfordn} shows that the number of scattered particles can be expressed as dependent on the foils thickness and atomic properties.
These properties are the only changing parameters in the equation, besides the atomic number.
For a chosen scattering angle $\theta$ the $Z$-dependence can be therefore expressed as
\begin{equation}
 \frac{R}{\rho_{N}\cdot d} \propto Z^2 \,.
 \label{eqn:Z_propto}
\end{equation}
To calculate the $Z$-dependence, the Rutherford experiment is done again for two foils made from bismuth and aluminium.
\subsubsection{Scattering count rate for bismuth foil}
The count rate depending on the scattering angle for scattering on a $d = \qty{1}{\micro\meter}$ bismuth foil is measured and displayed in the same way as for the gold foil scattering in \autoref{subsec:Au2}.
The original data is listed in \autoref{tab:Bi1} in the appendix.
\newline\noindent
To verify that the data follows the expectation of \autoref{eqn:rutherford}, the data is fitted with the fit-function \autoref{eqn:fit}.
\autoref{fig:Bi1} shows the count rate plotted against the scattering angle, as well as the fit-function.
\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{content/images/Bi1.pdf}
  \caption{Intensity of the $\alpha$-particles with respect to the scattering angle, scattered on $d_{\mathrm{Bi}} = \qty{1}{\micro\meter}$ bismuth foil.}
  \label{fig:Bi1}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier
\noindent
The grey data points are again not included for the fit because of them being within the measured influence of the incoming particle beam.
The parameters returned by the fit-function are
\begin{align}
  A_{\mathrm{Bi}} &= \qty{7.35(0.94)e-6}{\per\second} & b_{\mathrm{Bi}} &= \qty{4.29(0.04)}\,.
\end{align}
%
\subsubsection{Scattering count rate for Aluminium foil}
The thickness of the aluminium foil is $d_{\mathrm{Al}} = \qty{3}{\micro\meter}$.
As for the bismuth foil, the count rate depending on the scattering angle is plotted and fitted with \autoref{eqn:fit}.
Both is shown in \autoref{fig:Al3}. 
The original data is listed in \autoref{tab:Al3} in the appendix.
\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{content/images/Al3.pdf}
  \caption{Intensity of the $\alpha$-particles with respect to the scattering angle, scattered on $d_{\mathrm{Al}} = \qty{3}{\micro\meter}$ aluminium foil.}
  \label{fig:Al3}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier
\noindent
For this scattering experiment the highest achieved scattering angle is $\theta = \qty{9(0.1)}{\degree}$. 
After that point, the count rate reading was so small that the statistical error exceeds the acceptable range.
\newline\noindent
The grey data points are left out of the fit because of their high deviation from the other data points.
Furthermore, the fit-function did not converge when including the two data points. 
This is further discussed in \autoref{sec:Diskussion}.
\newline\noindent
The values of the parameters returned by the fit-function are
\begin{align}
  A_{\mathrm{Al}} &= \qty{2.42(0.55)e-6}{\per\second} & b_{\mathrm{Al}} &= \qty{4.31(0.06)}{}\,.
\end{align}
\subsubsection{Z-dependence for fixed angle}
As the fixed angle to verify the $Z$-dependence, $\theta = \qty{9(0.1)}{\degree}$ is chosen, because it is the largest angle measured for all three scattering materials.
The data is plotted according to the proportionality given in \autoref{eqn:Z_propto} and shown in \autoref{fig:Z_dependence}.
It is then fitted with a quadratic equation
\begin{equation}
  f\left(Z\right) = A\cdot Z^2 + B\,.
\end{equation}
\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{content/images/Z_Dependence.pdf}
  \caption{$\alpha$-particle intensity for gold, bismuth and aluminum, divided by the foil thickness $d$ and atomic density $\rho_{N}$, against the atomic number $Z$ for fixed $\theta = \qty{9(0.1)}{\degree}$. }
  \label{fig:Z_dependence}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier
\noindent
The parameters returned by the quadratic fit function are
\begin{align}
  A &= \qty{1.92(0.06)e-27}{\meter\squared\per\second} & B &= \qty{6.38(6.23)e-26}{\meter\squared\per\second}\,.
\end{align}
