\subsection{Rutherford scattering formula}
In the Rutherford scattering experiment alpha particles are scattered off a foil of different materials with the nuclear charge number $Z$. 
The scattering intensity is measured depending on the scattering angle $\theta$, wich describes the angle betwen the original particle beam an the path of a scattered particle behind the foil.
To derive a scattering formula the positive charge of an atom of the foil is assumed to be centered in the middle, as it is the case in Ruhterfors atomic model. 
Therefor the scattering of the positively charged alpha particles is asumed to be an elastic scattering process of the coulomb potential of the atom. 
The effective area for the elastic scattering is the differential cross section $\frac{\mathrm{d} \sigma}{\mathrm{d} \Omega }$, 
where $\mathrm{d} \Omega$ is the angular section, in wich the detector measures the scattered particles. 
It is assumed, that these effective areas do not overlap, which is sufficent for foils with up to $\num{10000}$ atomic layers. 
Under these assumptions differential cross section depending on the scattering angle is given by
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{d} \sigma = \frac{Z^2 e^4}{\left(2 \pi \epsilon_0\right)^2 E_{\mathrm{kin}}^2 } \frac{1}{\mathrm{sin}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)^4} \, \mathrm{d} \Omega \, .
    \label{eqn:rutherford}
\end{equation}

Here $e$ is the elementary charge, $e$ is the elementary charge and $E_{\mathrm{kin}}= \frac{1}{2} m v_0^2$ is the average kinetic energy of the alpha particles.
The rutherford formula can also be expressed in term of the number of particles $\mathrm{d}n$ scattered at a certain angle $\theta$ in the interval $\mathrm{d} \Omega$ relative to the total number of scattered particles $n$
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm{d} n}{n} = \frac{Z^2 e^2 d \rho_{N}}{\left(2 \pi \epsilon_0\right)^2 E_{\mathrm{kin}}^2 \mathrm{sin}\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)} \, \mathrm{d} \Omega \, .
    \label{eqn:rutherfordn}
\end{equation}
Here $d$ is the thickness of the foil, $\rho_N$ number of scattering centers per $\unit{\meter\cubed}$ \cite[39-44]{haken_1984}.


\subsection{Count rate}
In the experiment, multiple scattered particles are measured for a specific scattering angle. 
If the detector counts $N$ events in the time interval $\mathrm{\Delta}t$, the count rate $R$ is given by
\begin{equation}
    R = \frac{N}{\mathrm{\Delta} t} \, . 
    \label{eqn:countrate}
\end{equation}
Since $\frac{\mathrm{d} n}{n}$ is proportional to the count rate \cite[42]{haken_1984}
this count rate is also proportional to the cross section at an angle $\theta$. % and the angular section $\mathrm{d} \Omega$ of the detector \cite[39-44]{haken_1984}.
It follows that
\begin{equation} 
    R \left(\theta\right) \propto \frac{\mathrm{d} n}{n} \left(\theta\right) \propto \frac{\mathrm{d} \sigma}{\mathrm{d} \Omega} \left(\theta\right) \, .
    \label{eqn:RpropWQ}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Surface barrier detector} \label{subsec:detector}
The surface barrier detector, which is used in this experiment, functions as follwos: 
It has a junction of the semiconductor silicon and a metal. 
The silicon is p- or n-type doped, depending on the used metal.
Because of the different Fermi levels, the band layers of the silicon are lowered, so that a depletion zone is formed in the silicon.
Incoming ionizing radiation like alpha or gamma radiation dissipates energy by producing electron-hole pairs. 
These pairs recombine at the junction in the detector.
The resulting current is measured as an event.
The strengh of the current is proportional to the energy of the incoming particle. %so background from gamma radiation can be ruled out by setting a corresponding threshhold.
Since the band gap is around $\qty{1.1}{\electronvolt}$ wide, the detector is very sensitive to ambient light \cite[215-234]{leo_1994}.


\subsection{Uncertainties of the measurement}
\label{sec:fehlerrechnung}
To handle the uncertainties of the measurements the folowing formulas are used:
The number of measured events of a radioactive decay in a time interval follows the Poisson distribution. 
Therefore the uncertainty for $N$ measured events is given by $\mathrm{\Delta} N = \sqrt{N}$.
It follows that the relative uncertainty can be calculated using \cite[11]{leo_1994}
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\mathrm{\Delta}N}{N} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \, .
    \label{eqn:relative_poisson}
\end{equation}
\\
